# PD-105 — Company product, audience, culture, scale and markets

Status: Approved product direction on 11 September 2026; not implemented. This is the design input for
WM-008, WM-011 and later business/world tickets. Exact balance constants and complete regional content
remain execution work.

## Product identity

A product is descriptive and adaptive, not a rigid style class. Most fans primarily respond to stars,
stories, excitement, execution and value; small stylistic differences must not create arbitrary
penalties. Only sustained, material contradictions with established expectations have notable effects.

The model has three time horizons: **declared product** is the company's stated presentation;
**delivered product** is inferred from roughly eight to twelve important recent shows; **core identity**
is the slow-moving reputation built over years. Company core, brand identity and individual show
presentation are separate scopes. Advisors compare them and recommend changes; they never silently
rewrite the player's declaration after one unusual show.

Delivered product is inferred from independent tendencies: wrestling/angle emphasis, sporting
realism, story continuity, athletic complexity, violence, spectacle, comedy, match pace, finish
legitimacy and production polish. Generated names such as Sports Entertainment or Traditional Lucha
summarise combinations; they do not restrict booking. Major repositioning takes time, resources,
roster fit and owner authority. Trust/authenticity reflects whether the company can consistently
deliver its promise. Era trends create opportunities and risks, never a universally correct product.

## Audience

Audience composition uses viewing motivations rather than intelligence labels or fixed demographic
stereotypes: mainstream/casual, character/story, in-ring enthusiast, traditional/regional,
family/youth, hardcore/alternative, loyal promotion follower and big-event/novelty viewer. Each market
has an editable baseline; the actual event audience blends market, company, distribution, price,
stars, show type and booking history. Groups may react differently, but overall crowd response remains
the primary signal.

Companies estimate group reactions from attendance, viewing retention, merchandise, surveys, social
discussion and journalists. Evidence has confidence based on company resources and data access; the
interface never claims perfect knowledge. Audience expectations adapt gradually.

A genuine local representative can gain contextual familiarity. Regional identity matters more in
strongly local/traditional markets. A known international visitor can receive a novelty/special-
attraction reaction; an unknown outsider receives none. In a local promotion, local familiarity is
already normal and does not inflate every roster member. Alignment, story and promotion loyalty can
override hometown affection.

## Company culture and scale

Culture tracks leadership centralisation, formalisation, professionalism, accountability, medical/
physical safety, creative freedom, worker voice, loyalty/community, internal competitiveness and
openness to change. Size tends to increase specialisation, rules and management layers but does not
guarantee centralisation, competence, discipline or safety. Leadership, decisions, hiring, crises and
staff turnover change culture over time.

Scale labels are Micro, Local, Regional, National, International, Global and Global Powerhouse. Scale
derives from sustained audience and geographic reach, not cash or one popularity threshold. Regional
popularity, live reach, broadcast/streaming reach, cultural reach, business infrastructure, prestige,
momentum and financial health remain separate facts. Growth unlocks opportunities while increasing
staffing, governance, production and safety expectations.

Worker popularity is 0–100 per region and remains separate from wrestling ability, current-style
Overall, momentum, marketability and company reach. Interfaces show the region relevant to the
current decision as the headline value and provide the regional breakdown on demand. Drawing power is
a contextual outcome of these separate facts, not another editable universal rating.

Product disagreements, audience decline and failure against authorised owner goals may contribute to
warnings, reduced authority, demotion or dismissal. WM-030/PD-115 owns career roles and firing rules.
A controlling owner instead loses control through applicable board, bankruptcy, acquisition or
takeover systems.

## Geography and market behaviour

Canonical hierarchy: **World → Area → Region → Metro/City → Venue**. The default areas cover United
States, Canada, Mexico/Central America, Caribbean, South America, British Isles, Europe, Middle East/
North Africa, Sub-Saharan Africa, Japan, South Asia, East Asia, Southeast Asia and Oceania. Stable IDs
and editable catalogues allow custom databases to subdivide or extend them.

Regions provide population, economy, wrestling interest, audience tendencies, languages, travel
links, media access, local traditions and weighted neighbouring-market connections. These are editable
world facts, not hard-coded cultural judgments. Demand combines potential demand, awareness, current
interest, accessibility and event appeal. Broadcast exposure creates awareness without guaranteeing
ticket demand or loyalty. Popularity can spill across connected markets; prolonged absence cools
active interest without deleting historical recognition.

Live-event saturation is local and event-sensitive. A national company can produce weekly shows in
different cities without company-wide burnout. Repeated visits, repeated matchups, weak value or too
many similar events in the same market create temporary saturation. Regional preference, wrestling
interest and industry strength can evolve through successful companies and important stars.

## World generation, saves and venue boundary

Every region exists in every career. World Size offers Small, Standard and Large population density.
Simulation Detail offers Standard and Detailed, with optional advanced regional control. Player and
important areas receive full simulation; standard areas receive normal condensed development;
background areas aggregate activity until relevance increases. Detail affects density and processing,
not whether geography exists.

The shipped database is read-only. Custom database/scenario copies are editable before career start.
The career changes through simulation; a future everything-editor may directly alter it while marking
the save Edited/Sandbox and recording an audit history. Stable IDs and validation protect references.
Presentation asset packs can change media without changing simulation identity.

PD-105 owns a venue's stable geographic identity and market. PD-116/WM-033 owns venue types,
configurations, capacity, hire cost, availability, ownership, production constraints, travel, weather,
ticketing and scheduling. WM-034 owns media territories; WM-014 consumes presentation metadata.

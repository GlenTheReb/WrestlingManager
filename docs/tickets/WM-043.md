# WM-043 — Optional retro 3D broadcast engine

Status: Conditional future capability; accepted direction, not implementation-ready. It cannot delay
the core management game or WM-013/014. Reserved branch stem: `feature/wm-043-retro-broadcast`.

## Player promise

Watch canonical WM matches, entrances and later scenes as a lightweight low-poly broadcast while
retaining identical results, reports and an accessible text/2.5D fallback.

## System contract

- Owns the original PlayCanvas Engine v2 render layer, integrated through `@playcanvas/react` in the
  existing React/TypeScript/Vite/Tauri client, plus modular character assembly, animation playback,
  cameras, arena/crowd rendering and renderer diagnostics.
- Requires WebGL2 as the baseline renderer. WebGPU may improve presentation where reliably supported
  but cannot become a requirement or produce different canonical behavior.
- Consumes versioned WM-013 events and validated WM-041 assets; never calls simulation mutation paths.
- Emits playback-local state and diagnostics only. It cannot create results, ratings, injuries,
  popularity, history or save facts.
- After an accepted live amendment, it may discard and rebuild only buffered future animation choices
  from new WM-013 events; already shown actions remain immutable and animation callbacks never deliver
  instructions.

## Planned delivery programme

1. Licence/provenance audit and a go/no-go spike with one arena, two generic wrestlers, five paired
   moves, body variation, automated cameras and one stored timeline.
2. Canonical WM skeleton, modular model/attire generator, reproducible Blender-to-GLB processing,
   validation, loading, caching, level of detail, crowd instancing and hardware fallback.
3. Versioned WM-owned paired-interaction recipes, synchronised attacker/receiver animation ingestion,
   main move families, positioning, selling, recovery, reversals/interruptions and missing-move fallback.
4. Complete show playback: entrances, matches, interference, post-match actions, lighting/effects,
   commentary/crowd cues, camera direction, Quick Sim handoff, Extended Highlights, Realtime, seeking
   and playback speeds.
5. After core quality is proven, staged WM-029 promos/angles, representative tag/multi-person and
   stipulation support, optional validated model/arena/animation packs and creator tooling.

These are internal phases of one coherent future capability, not permission to create branches or
start implementation. The feasibility spike is a mandatory stop/go decision before the longer work.

## Invariants and exclusions

- Follow [PD-132](../retro-broadcast-engine-rules.md). Virtual Pro Grappler is reference/research only;
  no No Mercy/VPG code, unverified assets or unlicensed assets enter WM.
- The renderer must reproduce an existing timeline; it cannot invent a substitute outcome when an
  animation is unavailable.
- Text/2.5D remains complete and independently testable. Saves never embed renderer runtime state.
- 3D and 2.5D expose the same Live Direction decisions; renderer choice cannot change opportunity,
  delivery, worker response or replanning.
- No player-controlled combat, online match play, theme-song licensing or required video packs.
- A purchased pack is not approved until its precise commercial, conversion and redistribution terms
  are recorded; source assets remain outside public mod/export formats unless the licence permits them.

## Dependencies and scheduling

- A spike requires a stable match slice of WM-013 and existing canonical match facts.
- Main match playback consumes WM-007/010 move/finish metadata and WM-014 fallback behavior.
- Entrances/scenes consume WM-029; production presentation consumes WM-034; public packs consume
  WM-041; performance/accessibility gates consume WM-018.
- Planning estimate: 4–8 weeks for a convincing spike, 26–39 full-time engineering weeks for the main
  move-family/show engine, and approximately 52–78 weeks for broad release-quality polish.
- The spike must sustain 60 FPS within a 16.7 ms frame budget on a documented ordinary-PC baseline in
  the real Tauri/WebView2 client at its target resolution and preset. Record frame-time evidence and
  representative arena, crowd, lighting, wrestler and paired-move load; do not infer this from a browser
  demo or WebGPU-only result.

## Capability exit

Representative singles and tag shows replay deterministically across supported body types, speeds,
camera modes, missing-asset states and target hardware; the documented ordinary-PC preset sustains
60 FPS, and every frame remains subordinate to the same canonical events used by text playback and
reports.
